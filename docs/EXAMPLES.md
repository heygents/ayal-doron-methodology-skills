# Usage examples — Ayal Doron Methodology Skills

Worked examples of how to run the seven `/0N-win-*` skills, alone and as a chain, for a **business**,
a **team**, and a **person**. Prompts are shown the way users actually type them (Hebrew or English —
the skills answer in whichever language you use). Outputs are abbreviated and illustrative; a real run is
longer and asks you questions along the way.

> Everything here is built on the work of **Dr. Ayal Doron** — the anchor, the four tools and the examples
> (the five weddings, the key for every newborn, Netflix vs Blockbuster) are his. For his books, lectures and
> workshops: https://www.eyaldoron.co.il/

## 1. Quick start: the full chain on a business challenge

**Situation:** a SaaS company for freelance designers is losing its best customers to a bigger competitor.

### Step 1 — intake

```
/00-win-challenge-intake
אנחנו מאבדים את הלקוחות הכי טובים שלנו למתחרה גדול יותר. המטרה: להוריד churn ב-20% ברבעון הבא.
```

The skill asks a few questions per turn (who is this for, what was tried, constraints), runs the
**7-second test** ("במה העסק הכי מעולה בעולם? שבע שניות"), asks where you are on the uncertainty ladder,
and writes a Challenge Card. Abbreviated card:

```markdown
# Challenge Card — Losing top customers to BigCo
Scope: business · Owner: CEO
## The challenge (verbatim)
> "להוריד churn ב-20% ברבעון הבא"
## Classification
- Wording type: same-pencil goal (reduce)
## Anchor (7-second test)
- Answer: "אנחנו היחידים שעונים ללקוח תוך 10 דקות, בכל שעה" · Speed: fast · Named by others? yes
- Status: clear
## Uncertainty ladder: 2 (cope)
## Route
- Start with: /02-win-almost-absurd-goal — "reduce churn 20%" is a talent question; the goal must change first.
- Then: /03 → /05 → /06
```

### Step 2 — extremize the goal

```
/02-win-almost-absurd-goal
```

(no arguments needed — it reads the Challenge Card). The escalation ladder it builds with you:

```
1. Churn down 20%                                  (same pencil)
2. Customers stay and bring a friend               (still same pencil)
3. Every designer in the country wants to be here
4. Designers leave BigCo *because* of us
5. ✅ Designers pay us before they have their first client — and BigCo's customers migrate on their own.
```

Then it redefines the primitives: *what is churn? what is a customer? what is "our product" for a
designer with no clients yet?* — and sketches three new-field directions (e.g. "we are the place a designer
gets their first client", which is impossible in the old field of "project-management tool").

### Step 3 — extremize the obstacle

```
/03-win-extreme-obstacle
```

Obstacle as stated: "BigCo has 10x our marketing budget." Multiplied: *100x budget, and they give the product
away for free.* Breaking point: competing on features or price is dead. Redefined goal: "what does the
designer actually need on Monday morning?" → a paying client, not a better tool. Solution ladder:
extravagant (we buy the clients: a paid marketplace), moderate (we guarantee the first client or refund),
nearly free (we publish every designer's portfolio to a client list weekly).

### Step 4 — the knockout

```
/05-win-calculated-knockout
```

Supreme goal: "every designer earns a living from their craft." Field map: *already taken* = features
and price; *customer pays/suffers* = months without a first client; *come to them* = bring the client to
the designer. Candidate with a hindsight sentence: **"we guarantee your first paying client in 30 days or
the year is free — in two years everyone will ask why BigCo didn't do it."** Downside in numbers (how many
will claim the refund?), what BigCo cannot copy (their sales team sells to enterprises, not designers),
and a pilot: *only the Tel Aviv cohort, only March, kill signal: refund rate above 15%.*

### Step 5 — habits

```
/06-win-habit-infrastructure
```

Weekly sharpening of the anchor (10-minute response), a quarterly "not-like-us" advisor, the 90-minute
isolation rule after a lost deal, and the stop/keep/forget list.

## 2. Each skill on its own

Every skill runs without a Challenge Card. It asks the two or three questions it needs.

### `/00-win-challenge-intake` — when you don't know which tool you need

```
/00-win-challenge-intake
I'm a VP R&D. My team is good but I feel replaceable — every quarter there's a new AI tool that does part of my job.
```

It will not solve anything. It classifies the challenge, runs the 7-second test, and routes — here to
`/01`, because the anchor is unclear.

### `/01-win-irreplaceable-anchor` — "what am I the best in the world at?"

```
/01-win-irreplaceable-anchor
בשביל אחד ממנהלי הצוותים שלי. הוא מצוין אבל לא יודע להגיד במה.
```

Expect the three forbidden answers to be rejected ("no one is irreplaceable"), 5–8 candidates from three
angles (the room / the pattern / the human residue), a scoring table, and one anchor sentence:

> "דנה היא זו שמגיעה לעובדים שאף אחד אחר לא מצליח לדבר איתם, בכל הארגון."

plus a weekly sharpening ritual and the "call Ruti" test as evidence.

For a business, phrase it as the business:

```
/01-win-irreplaceable-anchor
For our company: a boutique accounting firm, 12 people, competing with big firms and with AI bookkeeping.
```

### `/02-win-almost-absurd-goal` — rewrite a modest goal

```
/02-win-almost-absurd-goal  How do we make our onboarding less confusing?
```

Output: the goal named as "same pencil", an escalation ladder ("a new user cries when the onboarding
ends"; "users finish onboarding before they sign up"), the five quality tests, primitives to redefine
(what is onboarding? what is "done"?), and three new-field directions.

### `/03-win-extreme-obstacle` — multiply the obstacle

```
/03-win-extreme-obstacle
יש לנו רק שני אנשי תמיכה ו-400 טיקטים בשבוע.
```

Output: escalation (2 people / 4,000 tickets; then zero support people), the breaking point, the coffee
pause if you try to quit, the redefined goal ("what does the customer actually want when they open a
ticket?"), and the three-level solution ladder.

### `/04-win-extreme-persona` — "1 + someone's idea"

```
/04-win-extreme-persona
Problem: nobody reads our internal weekly update. Consult: MrBeast and a kindergarten teacher.
```

Output: two contrasting 60-second concepts, each one's "chicken for a shekel" (MrBeast: one number in
the subject line that changes every week; the teacher: a sticker for whoever answers first), and your
translation into a single-message move.

### `/05-win-calculated-knockout` — the move that's obvious in hindsight

```
/05-win-calculated-knockout
We're a 3-person coffee-cart chain. The incumbent is a 200-branch cafe chain.
```

Output: the supreme-goal question, the field map, 3–5 candidates each with a "why didn't we…" sentence,
honest downside, and a pilot scoped to one location and one month.

### `/06-win-habit-infrastructure` — make it stick

```
/06-win-habit-infrastructure
I want to become someone who enjoys uncertainty instead of surviving it.
```

Output: 3–5 habits with cadence and environment change, a contagion protocol, stop/keep/forget, and a
90-day check.

## 3. A team challenge

**Situation:** a product team of eight keeps shipping small improvements; leadership wants "innovation".

```
/00-win-challenge-intake
אנחנו צוות מוצר של 8. ההנהלה רוצה "חדשנות" אבל כל רבעון אנחנו משפרים את הקיים. מטרה: פיצ'ר אחד פורץ דרך השנה.
```

Routing: `/02` (the goal "one breakthrough feature" is still a talent question). Suggested prompts after the card:

```
/02-win-almost-absurd-goal
```
> ladder ends at: "users cancel their competitor's subscription on the day we ship, without us telling them to."

```
/04-win-extreme-persona  Consult Jony Ive, a Formula-1 pit crew chief, and a 9-year-old.
```
> three concepts in 60 seconds each; the pit-crew "one thing" ("every second of the user's first minute is rehearsed") becomes the team's single message.

```
/06-win-habit-infrastructure
```
> for a team: rotate the "not-like-us" reviewer seat monthly, a weekly 2% on the anchor moment (the demo), and the 90-minute rule after a failed launch.

## 4. A personal challenge

**Situation:** a 42-year-old marketing manager, afraid of being replaced by AI tools.

```
/00-win-challenge-intake
אני מנהלת שיווק, 42. חצי ממה שאני עושה AI כבר עושה. מה אני עושה עם זה?
```

Expect the 7-second test to come first. Then:

```
/01-win-irreplaceable-anchor
```
> anchor: "היא זו שמדביקה את כל החדר באופטימיות כשהקמפיין נופל, בכל החברה." · weekly sharpening: the Monday standup.

```
/02-win-almost-absurd-goal  My goal: stay relevant in marketing for the next 5 years.
```
> ladder ends at: "in three years, companies hire me to be the person the AI tools consult."

```
/06-win-habit-infrastructure
```
> a radically different new friend every three months, immersion (every campaign brief written with the new tool by default), and "what I stop doing".

The same flow works for a parent asking about a teenager: run `/01` with the child as the subject
(strengths only), then `/06` for the family habit infrastructure — see the relocation and English examples
in [`references/ayal-doron-examples.md`](../references/ayal-doron-examples.md).

## 5. Using the skills inside a workshop or offsite

Ayal's original format was a lecture followed by 45-minute table rounds. To reproduce it with the skills:

1. Before the day, each table lead runs `/00-win-challenge-intake` on the table's topic and brings the Challenge Card.
2. Round 1 (45 min): `/02-win-almost-absurd-goal`, then `/03-win-extreme-obstacle`. Ask each table for **a question, not a solution**.
3. Round 2: `/04-win-extreme-persona` with two personas the table picks and one opposite the facilitator adds.
4. Round 3: `/05-win-calculated-knockout` — each table pitches one move with its hindsight sentence and a pilot.
5. Close: `/06-win-habit-infrastructure` for the team that owns the follow-up.

Paste the table's raw notes into the prompt; the skills work from messy input.

## 6. Where outputs go

If the workspace has an `Outputs/` folder, each skill saves to `Outputs/Win/<challenge-slug>/`:

```
Outputs/Win/losing-top-customers/
  challenge-card.md          ← /00
  01-anchor.md               ← /01
  02-almost-absurd-goal.md   ← /02
  03-extreme-obstacle.md     ← /03
  04-extreme-persona.md      ← /04
  05-knockout.md             ← /05
  06-habit-infrastructure.md ← /06
```

Later skills read earlier files automatically. Without an `Outputs/` folder, the skills work in chat and
offer to save.

## 7. Tips and anti-patterns

- **Give the goal as you actually phrase it today.** The wording is data; `/02` needs the "modest" version to escalate from.
- **Don't bring a solution to `/02` or `/03`.** If you do, they will recover the goal behind it first — that costs a turn.
- **Let the 7-second test be seven seconds.** A slow or generic answer is itself the finding.
- **Combine `/02` and `/03`** when one alone doesn't move you — "five weddings *and* talked about for four years" is what produced Ayal's whole solution ladder.
- **Use `/04` for speed**, not for taste. Pick personas you would never agree with; the lens works regardless.
- **A knockout without a hindsight sentence is an improvement.** `/05` will say so; take it as a signal to go back to `/02`.
- **Run `/06` last, always.** A direction without habit infrastructure fades within a quarter.
