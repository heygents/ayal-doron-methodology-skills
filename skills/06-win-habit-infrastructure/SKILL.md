# 06 · Win — Habit Infrastructure (תשתית של הרגלים)

> "If you have goals, always build a habit infrastructure between you and your wants and goals."
> "Either we infect each other upward, or downward. It's mental — and it comes with hard work."

The tools produce a direction. Without infrastructure the direction fades. Ayal wanted kids who are good
at change, so the family moved every four years and switched schools every two; he wants mental flexibility,
so every three to six months he adds a friend who is radically different from him. **Decide what you want,
then build the environment that makes it routine.**

## Read first

- `../../references/ayal-doron-examples.md` §8 (habits, contagion, presence) and §1 (what to stop doing).
- `Outputs/Win/<slug>/` outputs, especially `01-anchor.md` and whatever direction was chosen. Otherwise ask: what do you want to become good at (as a person / team / business), and what is the anchor.

## Language

User's language (default Hebrew). Keep: תשתית של הרגלים, השחזה, "הפסד מדבק".

## Workflow

### 1) Name the want in one line
"I / we want to be good at ___." Examples: change and uncertainty · mental flexibility · being the ones people
call for X · shipping calculated risks. Tie it to the anchor and to the ladder level they're aiming for (usually 3, thrive).

### 2) Design 3–5 habits, each with a cadence and an environment change
Use Ayal's patterns as prompts; adapt the scale to the user (a team can't move cities, but it can rotate rooms, roles, customers):

| Pattern | Ayal's version | Team / business version |
|---|---|---|
| **Sharpen the anchor** | "every week I improve that meeting a little more" | a weekly 2% on the unit where the anchor shows |
| **Scheduled disruption** | move city every 4 years, new school every 2 | rotate ownership / seating / customer segment on a calendar, not when forced |
| **Exposure to difference** | a radically different new friend every 3–6 months | one customer, partner or advisor per quarter who is nothing like you; a "not-like-us" seat in reviews |
| **Immersion, not lessons** | YouTube switches to English after 20 minutes | the skill becomes the default medium (all demos in the new tool, all reviews with the new question) |
| **Multiple languages / arts / sports until 15** | breadth before depth | cross-training between functions on a cadence |
| **Presence** | "whoever texts while present — done" | phones-down rule for the anchor moment |

For each habit: **trigger / cadence · the smallest version · who is in the environment · what it costs**.

### 3) Contagion rules (מה מדבק)
Hope, optimism and pessimism are all contagious. Write the team's or family's protocol, modeled on the judo
national team:
- **After a loss:** who isolates, for how long (Ayal: 90 minutes), so the loss doesn't infect others — including the leader when *their* person loses.
- **After a win:** how it is spread deliberately.
- **Who is the carrier:** the one person whose mood sets the room; agree what they do on a bad day.

### 4) What to stop doing (AI-era questions)
From the opening of the lecture, ask and answer in one line each:
- What do I / we **stop doing**?
- Which knowledge is **still critical**? Which should we **forget**?
- Which limits — including mental ones — **no longer exist**?

### 5) 90-day check
Set one date and one observable per habit ("someone said 'call X' unprompted", "we ran the pilot in the south",
"the new friend/advisor met us twice"). Keep it to a single short table.

## Guardrails

- Habits must be small enough to survive a bad month. If a habit needs willpower every time, redesign the environment instead.
- Family examples are Ayal's; when the user applies this to their own children, keep suggestions age-appropriate and let the parent decide.
