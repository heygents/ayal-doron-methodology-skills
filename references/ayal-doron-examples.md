# Ayal Doron — methodology reference and examples bank

Source: Dr. Ayal Doron's lecture at Elementor Day (9 Sep 2026), "how to be unreplaceable" —
a toolbox for a person, a team, or a business. All ideas and examples are his; when a user asks where this
comes from, credit Dr. Ayal Doron and point to https://www.eyaldoron.co.il/ . This file is the shared reference for all
`/0N-win-*` skills. Skills quote from it; do not invent examples and attribute them to Ayal.

Hebrew key phrases are kept because users usually know the ideas by these words.

---

## 1. Core thesis

- AI is not "faster" — it is a structural change. It improves ~2x every four months. It is already
  "smarter than all of us"; the right question is **in what is it *not*?** (במה מותר האדם).
- Questions AI forces on you: *What do I stop doing? Which knowledge is still critical? Which
  knowledge should I forget? What limits — including mental limits — no longer exist?*
- **Whoever feels replaceable is the first to be replaced** in a world where machines replace tasks.
  Whoever searches for what they are irreplaceable in walks a different path on the same time and effort.
- The three human strongholds he names: emotional intelligence (which AI already fakes very well),
  love/connection, and **gut feeling / intuition** — "to stir everything I ever learned and not even be
  able to explain it". Intuition and **presence** (AI runs 5–50 million conversations in parallel; it is
  never truly present) are the raw material of the new playing field.
- **Predictable question → predictable answer from the same playing field. An extreme, almost-absurd
  question forces you to invent a new playing field on the same half hour of thinking.**
  (שאלה צפויה מייצרת תשובה צפויה. שאלה כמעט מופרכת מאלצת המצאת מגרש חדש.)
- **Talent vs super-talent** (טלנט / סופר-טלנט): a talent sharpens the same pencil (מחדד את אותו עיפרון)
  — faster, less bureaucracy, two vouchers instead of one. A super-talent refuses to ask the same
  question and changes the question so that a new field is the only possible answer.
- **Give me a question, not a solution** (אל תיתן לי פתרון, תן לי שאלה). Talents rush to solutions.
- "From zero to one, not more of the same."

## 2. The uncertainty ladder (four levels)

| Level | Relationship to uncertainty and change | Earns | Who they become |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Avoids** uncertainty and change ("routine, don't overtake, you'll get there") | barely | at risk of unemployment |
| 2 | **Copes** with uncertainty — gets up and does it because they are diligent, responsible, have no choice | fine | talent |
| 3 | **Thrives** on uncertainty — enjoys change and crises, smiles when there is something to crack ("the slightly annoying ones") | 10x | super-talent |
| 4 | **Creates** uncertainty — seeks it, generates it (calls you at 6:15 because they held back since 4:00) | founder | everyone else works for them |

Two people at level 1 and level 2 invest the same effort and intelligence; after five years one manages the other.
**The jump from 2 to 3 requires knowing what you are the best in the world at** — the anchor (עוגן).
"No one can cope with change without constantly working on their anchors."

## 3. The anchor: "what am I the best in the world at?" (במה אני הכי מעולה בעולם)

- "The first tool to prevent unemployment and ensure prosperity is answering this question. It took me
  five years to understand why the [very successful] answer it differently than we do."
- **The three forbidden answers** — the matrix that was put in our heads:
  1. "No one is irreplaceable."
  2. "Cemeteries are full of people who were sure they were irreplaceable."
  3. "If I'm irreplaceable, it's at home — for my kids / parents / partner."
  These come from a script that says the question is immodest, narcissistic, "you live in a movie".
  Humility is staying alert, curious, in a constant process of improvement, with proportion — not
  ignoring your distinct strength.
- **Scale:** "world" does not mean the universe. It means your organization, your neighborhood,
  the people around your table.
- **It is self-precision, not self-fullness** (דיוק עצמי, לא מלאות עצמית).
- **The 7-second test:** he asked two co-founders "what is he best at? what is she best at?" — the
  answers came within seven seconds, without "let me think". If you know your anchor in seven
  seconds you also know who completes you and which problems to bring to whom.
- **Examples of anchors (there are "1,200 options"):**
  gives the insight that changes the whole picture · solves problems · makes things happen ·
  infects everyone with optimism · brings courtesy into every room they enter · reaches the employees
  nobody else can talk to · runs team meetings where everyone participates.
- **"Call Ruti" test:** you know it worked when in a meeting someone says "wait, wait — if it's about
  technology and representation, call Ruti. That's Ruti." Ruti put it in everyone's head and everyone
  put it in Ruti's head. Ruti just jumped from level 2 to level 3.
- **Sharpen it weekly:** "If I'm the best at running team meetings where everyone participates, every
  week I improve that meeting a little more, I hone it. Then I know who I am and I'm not afraid of change."
- **Relocation story:** a family moving abroad, 15-year-old afraid of the change. Ayal asked one
  question only: what is the kid best in the world at? Football (soccer). Second question: which US
  state? — check whether it's a soccer state or an American-football state. It was football country.
  The kid trained 4.5 months in American football before moving. "An athlete is an athlete." He arrived
  with his anchor and became popular. When you have the thing you are best at and you cultivate it, you
  jump from level 2 to level 3.
- The anchor is what you *bring to the table* when "everything can be replaced".

## 4. Tool 1 — The almost-absurd goal (הקצנת המטרה / מטרה כמעט מופרכת)

**Rule:** you never leave a goal the way you received it. You always turn it into an almost-absurd goal.
The ambitious goal is *easier* to reach than the modest one, because it leaves you no choice but to
invent a new field.

| Goal as received (talent asks) | Almost-absurd goal (super-talent asks) | What it forces |
|---|---|---|
| How do we close the gaps in Israel's housing crisis? (→ less bureaucracy, faster committees) | **How does every baby born in Israel receive a key to an apartment on the day of birth?** | After three minutes you must ask: what is housing? what is a key? what is an apartment? what is ownership? what is property? — and rethink from primitives. |
| How do we make school less boring? (→ active recess, orange walls, gamification "from 1970") | **What does school have that no home has — even the most wonderful home?** (the kid *cries* when told to go home, despite a great family, siblings, friends, nine screens and candy) | Defining the uniqueness of school over every variation of home. |
| How do we retain our best employees? (→ two vouchers instead of one, two fun days) | The room's escalation: employees bring friends (still the same pencil) → all the talents in the country want to work here → people outside the company recommend it → **the best talents in the country fight to work here** → **people pay to work here** | Smiling more and giving attention won't do it; you must change the whole event. |
| How do my kids learn English dramatically better? (→ enroll in a class, drive, forget, "next week") | **My kids will be native English speakers by age ten.** | YouTube switches to English after 20 minutes, no translation. Three weeks later the six-year-old cursed him fluently in English for five minutes. "I invented a new field for learning English. That's all I did." |

Two groups, both serious, both smart: group 1 gets the left question, group 2 gets the right question.
The gap is **20x in creativity**; it would be embarrassing to watch the pitches.

**Quality checks Ayal applied live:**
- "Don't give me the solution, give me the goal / the question."
- "Bringing friends can still be an improvement of the same pencil — keep going, it's hard now, give me more."
- The goal must be so big that there is nothing to hurry toward; you *have* to invent.

**Escalation grammar** (how his examples are built):
- "לא רק ש… אלא ש…" — *not only do they stay, they bring friends; not only bring friends, the best fight to come.*
- "כל X מקבל Y ביום Z" — *every baby gets a key on the day of birth.*
- "מה יש ב-A שאין בשום B, גם ב-B הכי מופלא" — *what does A have that no B has, even the best B?*
- "אנשים משלמים כדי…" / "X הולכים מכות כדי…" — *people pay to… / X fight over…*
- "דוברי שפת אם עד גיל…" — *native-level by age…*

## 5. Tool 2 — The extreme obstacle (הקצנת המכשול)

**Rule:** don't just improve execution against the obstacle you have — enlarge the obstacle on purpose
until the current approach obviously collapses, and the mind is forced to design a new kind of solution.

- "If four students disrupt the class you'll never solve it. If it were 25 students and you were
  convinced the whole class won't let you teach, you'd go out and solve it from the root."
- "If I'm short four engineers I do nothing. If I'm short 25 engineers, I do something."
- "Three terrorists — same fence. Thirty — same fence. Three hundred — same fence. Three *thousand*
  — 'wait, what *is* a fence? show me the fence.'" The brain forgets to exaggerate; exaggeration is what
  gets us out of where we are.

**The two weddings:** a man has two important weddings next Wednesday, both at 20:00, 2.5 hours apart.
"And don't tell me to go to only one." The group-1 question: *how do I be at both?* → answer: a bit and a bit.
Super-talent move: **five weddings next Wednesday — one in Eilat, one in the Galilee, three of your choice —
and four years later everyone still talks about the fact that you were there.** (obstacle × goal combined)

The solution ladder that the extreme obstacle produced, from expensive to nearly free — each one keeps the
*redefined* goal ("be unforgettable at five weddings"), not the original one ("be physically present"):

1. **Helicopter** (~100K ILS) — land inside the chuppah. Nobody forgets who the maniac with the helicopter was.
2. **A disproportionate check** (~10K) — the friend is so moved he keeps inventing memories of you dancing
   at a wedding you never attended.
3. **A five-shekel check** — nobody forgets you either (not in a good way — but the mind now runs on constraints).
4. **Beni's method** (250 ILS) — "an architect of solutions": ask *what does a groom actually do at a
   wedding?* Dances, drinks, dances, drinks, faints. So pay someone to go to the dance floor once an hour
   and tell the groom "where are you? Beni was just looking for you". Four times. In the morning the groom
   remembers one word: Beni.
5. **AI photographer** — a good photographer plants you in the wedding photos.

"Look how many options were born from extremizing the problem. That's what you are going to do."
**The coffee pause:** when the person says "you're complicating it", Ayal goes to get coffee. "If you believe
in the process, wait." Nine out of ten leave; the one who stays gets the solution.

If both tools fail, combine them (five weddings + talked about for four years) — and if it still fails, go to Tool 3.

## 6. Tool 3 — The extreme persona ("1 + someone's idea") (דמות קיצונית)

**Formula:** *one problem + the idea of someone bold, extreme, colorful = a solution within 60 seconds.*
Imagine that person is here and ask: what would they tell us to do? You don't argue whether they are
right or good — you take inspiration from someone who "lives in extremes, in speeds, very distinct in
their thinking".

- **Trump appointed principal of the school** — within 60 seconds you have the whole concept: mahogany,
  gold stripes, a teacher fired every four days, rhetoric, economics, jacket and tie, a stage. "You can
  literally walk the corridors." Then swap: **Gandhi as principal** — only mats, no wood, no gold, no bells,
  no punishments, no stages, no jackets, everyone empathy, everyone hugged. Also walkable in 60 seconds.
- **Trump takes over the safari park** — his first three steps are obvious: a fur shop at the entrance (to
  create protests), a hunting range, five brunch tracks, an ecosystem of kosher slaughter. **Gandhi at the
  safari** — you were only at the safari if you hugged the cow and kissed the elephant's tusk.
- **Local authority problem:** "we work very hard and residents are ungrateful — the moment the garbage
  truck wakes them, the mayor is late." Ask them: whom do you want to consult? → **Rami Levy** (Israeli
  discount-supermarket founder; famous for chicken for one shekel). *1 + chicken-for-a-shekel = residents
  thank us even when the truck wakes them.* So the only question: **what is your "chicken for a shekel"
  for the resident?** In Tel Aviv: free parking. The truck wakes him at 6:00 and he sends hearts to the
  mayor through the noise. Rami Levy's lesson: *the brain can hold one thing. Don't multiply messages.
  If you don't get that, you haven't understood business.*
- Use politicians and public figures "not to argue politics, but to say: if they were here, what would
  they do to crush every competitor you haven't invented yet."

## 7. Tool 4 — The calculated risk / knockout (סיכון מחושב / נוקאאוט)

**Knockout** = "I bring not only irreplaceable value — I bring it with such force and speed that nobody
else can compete with me" (apropos go-to-market). **Calculated risk** = a move that sounds crazy in real
time and in hindsight everyone says "why didn't *we* do that?" Very often the move is already lying here;
you just aren't taking it.

- **Netflix vs Blockbuster.** Netflix worth $50M, Blockbuster $50B with 9,000 stores. Reed Hastings
  offered to sell for $50M; they threw him out; he said "we'll take them apart". The store playing field
  was *already taken* — opening 5 or 10 stores is the same pencil. So: *you go to Blockbuster; I'll come to
  your home.* DVDs by mail. *You pay late fees at Blockbuster; I give you a subscription with no fees* —
  19.90 for the subscription, the "free" is the no-fees. Within a week there were two companies: one you go
  to, one that comes to you. Then streaming, then a production company, then **binge**: managers said
  "we'll go bankrupt — we spent a third of the annual budget on a series and let them watch it in one
  night." Hastings asked: *what is our supreme goal as a company?* — "the best entertainment for every
  person in the way they want to consume it." *Then if they want it in one sequence, give it in one
  sequence — and give more freedom and control than any competitor ever.*
  "That's when I understood the formula: take a calculated risk that others don't think of taking."
- **Amazon free shipping** — early on nobody ordered; shipping was $5–7. The hotel insight: you pay
  thousands for the room and want to file a class action over an 18-shekel bottle of water in the lobby.
  So: free shipping. Managers: "you're insane, we'll go bankrupt." Bezos: they'll order 3x, 5x.
- **Bank Leumi** — customer-relations surveys favored a competitor. The "crazy" idea: put the **branch
  manager's name, photo and personal phone number** in the advertising. Someone objected: "they won't
  let them work." Someone asked: how many will actually call? — "thirty". Thirty calls, in exchange for a
  risk that in hindsight everyone asks "why wasn't it us?"
- **Bold idea, cautious execution.** "The idea can be daring; its execution can be more careful. Only in
  the south, only in south-east Ramat Gan — I'm testing it. Calculated in the test, bold in the idea. If
  it works, I scale."
- The eight "platinum" people in his book (Hastings, MrBeast, Jacinda Ardern, Oprah, Jony Ive, Reid
  Hoffman, the "No Sugar Lady", Carlo Petrini of Slow Food) each have almost-absurd goals, exaggerated
  self-imposed obstacles — and each searches for their **next knockout**. "Your knockout is what gave birth
  to you [as a company]. The question is what the *next* knockout is."

## 8. Habit infrastructure (תשתית של הרגלים)

**Rule:** "If you have goals, always build a habit infrastructure between you and your wants and goals."
Decide what you want; build the infrastructure accordingly.

- He wanted his kids good at change and uncertainty → every four years the family moves to a nearby city
  ("we and our movers are the regulars"), a new school every two years, three months a year in an
  international school in grades 8–9, as many languages / music styles / sports as they want until 15.
- He wants mental flexibility → **every three to six months a new friend who is radically different**
  from him: a prison commander, a settler from Samaria, a Breslov hasid. "If they were like me I wouldn't
  be mentally flexible." Israeli school teaches you only with people like you and calls it social skills;
  you cultivate foreignness toward anyone who isn't exactly like you.
- **Contagion:** hope is contagious, optimism is contagious, and so is pessimism. Israel's judo national
  coach (Oren Smadja) has a protocol: an athlete who loses isolates for 90 minutes so the loss doesn't
  infect the team; if *his* athlete loses, the coach removes *himself* for 90 minutes. "Either we infect
  each other upward or downward. It's mental, and it comes with hard work."
- Presence: "whoever texts while they're present — done. Either be there or leave."

## 9. How the tools chain (Ayal's own sequencing)

1. Know your anchor first — without it you cannot move fields (section 3).
2. Extremize the goal (Tool 1). If that doesn't move you —
3. Extremize the obstacle (Tool 2). If still stuck, combine 1 + 2 (five weddings + talked about for years).
4. If still stuck, call an extreme persona (Tool 3) — 60 seconds.
5. Then ask what the calculated-risk knockout is (Tool 4), anchored to the supreme goal.
6. Build the habit infrastructure so it holds (section 8).

Closing line: "You are irreplaceable, or the best in the world at something. You have to work for it —
ask, dig, sharpen. Look for new fields, take a goal and make it absurd, extremize the obstacle, call
someone crazy and think like them, look for our knockout through calculated risk, and build ourselves a
habit infrastructure we love."

**Shared almost-absurd goal (for groups, teams, nations):** a group that fights inside the existing field
cannot live together. It needs a goal so big and almost absurd that every member can find their place under
it — that is what "a unifying vision" means. Founding a state after 2,000 years was such a goal; after ~80
years of guarding it, a new one of the same size is needed.
